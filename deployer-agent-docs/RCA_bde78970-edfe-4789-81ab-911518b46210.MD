**Scope:** READ-ONLY diagnosis. No data was modified.

## Verdict (short)
Changing her role in Master Admin did **NOT** corrupt or reset her password, and she is **NOT** locked out. The role change was applied to her **operator `users`** record and, by design, **revoked her active session tokens** (`tokens_revoked_reason: "role_or_permissions_changed"`). That force-logged her out for the first time in days and forced a fresh password login — which is failing. The "Invalid credentials" is a **credential/portal mismatch surfaced by the forced re-login** (causes **a + d**), not password corruption (b) and not lockout (c).

## Root cause
1. **The role change revoked her tokens (by design), forcing a fresh login.** She had been riding a long-lived session (last real password login 2026-08-27). The revocation ended that session, so for the first time in ~week she must re-type a password.
2. **On that forced login she cannot authenticate** — a pre-existing credential/portal problem that the live session had masked:
   - **(d) Portal confusion** — rate buckets show her hitting both the normal login and the master-admin login portal. Her two accounts take two different passwords.
   - **(a) No CRM cross-login fallback** — if she uses the **CRM portal** (`/api/crm/auth/login`), her `crm_users` row is **not** `cross_login_provisioned`, so the platform-password fallback never fires; she must use the separate CRM password Master Admin set at creation, not her Pulse/platform password.

**Answer to the owner's question:** Editing a user's role in Master Admin does **not** change or break their password. It **does** revoke their existing sessions (intended behaviour for a role/permission change), which forces a re-login. If the user doesn't have the exact right password for the exact right portal, that forced re-login shows as "Invalid credentials."

## What I could NOT determine (no data can)
- **Which password string she is actually typing** — plaintext isn't stored and bcrypt can't be reversed/tested here. Both stored hashes are valid, so the *correct* password for whichever portal will authenticate.

- If unified login is intended, set `cross_login_provisioned: true` on her `crm_users` row so the CRM portal falls back to her platform password; otherwise re-issue her the separate CRM password.
